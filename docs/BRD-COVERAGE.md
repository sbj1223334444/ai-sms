# What is built, and what is not

Mapped against the 15 user stories in the epic pack.

| Story | Built | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US-01 SSO and persona-based entry | Substituted | No corporate directory on this build, so sign-in is open: ten demo accounts plus a free-form option, switchable from the header. Department-based form filtering is fully enforced, on the server and on the URL. Swap the providers in `src/lib/auth.ts` for SSO and nothing else changes. |
| US-02 Forms and submission | Yes | All 13 forms rebuilt field by field from BRD section 6: every field, in the BRD's order, under the BRD's sections, with the BRD form codes (GIR, AIR, FAT, DG, LAS, RWI) and persona mapping. Conditional display (flight related, hazard type, evasive action, communication difficulties, second aircraft, vehicle), cascading hazard lists, repeating passengers, 10-digit phones, 24-hour times, past-only occurrence dates, minimum-length narratives and plain-language errors. The common fields (name, email, staff number, department) are filled in from the account at the top of every form, read-only, and disappear entirely once a report is marked confidential. The AIRPROX diagram sheet is built: the pilot marks the first sighting and the closest point of the other aircraft in plan and in elevation, and the marks are shown on the report and the printed extract. Fields the BRD fills from CAE after submission are noted, not asked. Attachments record metadata only — bytes are not stored yet. |
| US-03 Triage, assignment, queues | Yes | Four triage outcomes, safety reference issued on acceptance only, mandatory rejection comment, department-scoped queue. |
| US-04 Workspace and CAE | Partly | Five tabs for Investigation + SRA, four for SRA only. CAE is **not connected** — no access on this build — so crew and aircraft are entered by hand and stamped as manual. `src/lib/cae.ts` holds the shape and the rules for switching it on. Assignment means assignment: from triage the report belongs to the investigator or SMS nodal it was handed to, and only they - plus Admin and System Admin - fill in the investigation, the SRA, the stages, the tasks or the request for closure. Everyone else with access reads it. A gatekeeper can hand it to someone else from the report at any time, and the queue separates "Assigned to me" from the rest, with the holder on every row. Report editing (criteria 3, 12, 13) is built: on an Investigation + SRA report in progress, roles with `report.editSubmitted` can correct the reporter's answers; each changed field is on the Timeline with its old and new value, the original submission is kept and viewable, and identity and the confidentiality choice are locked. SRA-only reports are read-only. Admin notes are not built. |
| US-05 Investigation record | Yes | All of section 9, 15-finding cap. SRA tabs exist only where an assessment does: one per finding ticked "needs a safety risk assessment", one on an SRA-only report from the moment it is accepted, none on a database-only one. Unticking the flag or deleting the finding deletes that assessment and everything in it, after a warning that names what will be lost, and the Timeline records it. |
| US-06 SRA and 5x5 matrix | Yes | Every field of section 7: register entry (reference, serial, originator, source, ICAO location, date added, functional area and sub-function), hazards with description, root cause, resultant risk, worst credible effect and their existing and additional controls, then the action, owner, department, deadline and status, with review period, review date, effectiveness and action plan once it is closed. The risk index is one pre- and one post-mitigation rating for the assessment as a whole, not one per hazard - a second finding that needs assessing gets its own SRA tab and its own pair. Severity and probability rules enforced server-side, severity reduction needs explicit confirmation, residual risk above tolerance routes to the configured approver. Saving is always allowed; what is still missing comes back with the assessment. |
| US-07 Workflow stages | Yes, changed | Five default stages, target dates chained from the previous deadline. Tasks are raised from each stage card and the stage cannot complete while its tasks are open. Add, delete and reorder were out of scope in the BRD and are now in scope: see US-18 and US-19. A stage completed too early can be reopened with a reason by whoever holds the report: it goes back in progress with its work intact, every stage after it starts again, and the Timeline records why. |
| US-08 Tasks | Mostly | Raised from the Workflow tab (Ask for information, Add task, and Assign task on each stage card) for the four BRD purposes: reporter, form edit, anyone in the company, control or action. Assignee picked from the staff directory with search; the reporter is filled in for a reporter request and refused on a confidential report. Manual due dates; section-level edit access, and the assignee sees and edits only those sections. Comments, extension requests, and accept or send back with a mandatory comment. A raised task can be changed - title, assignee, due date, priority, including granting a requested extension - or deleted, which takes it off the report and stops it holding up its stage; both go on the Timeline and the assignee is told. Tasks show on the stage card they belong to as well as in the Tasks panel. Open tasks block their own stage and closure. Every task event is on the Timeline. Not built: task attachments (the app stores no file bytes yet), the due-date morning reminder (needs a scheduler), the staff portal. |
| US-09 Closure and two-stage approval | Yes | Readiness gate lists its blockers, gatekeeper then COFS, rejection reopens and can reassign. |
| US-10 Effectiveness monitoring | Partly | Review outcomes and reopening work. The 10/5/1/day-of reminder schedule needs a cron job — see ARCHITECTURE.md. |
| US-11 Notifications | Partly | Server-side notification layer with the privacy rule built in; logs to console until SMTP is set. In-app bell and 30-day history not built. |
| US-12 Extraction, export, APIs | Partly | Excel export complete with the section 10 column order, permission check, 10,000-row cap. Printable extract works. Dashboard APIs not exposed yet. |
| US-13 RBAC, groups, audit | Partly | Full matrix enforced server-side; audit is the git log when the GitHub store is on, and an in-process log in demo mode. Group editing is config-file only, no UI. |
| US-14 DMS repository | No | Not started. |
| US-15 Legacy data migration | No | Not started. Write it as a script against `src/lib/store`. |
| US-16 Build and edit report forms | Yes | Administration → Forms. Field builder with live preview and a JSON view for conditional branches; versions stamped on each report; retire instead of delete. Shipped forms open and save back unchanged (checked in `npm run check`). |
| US-17 Control who sees each form | Yes | All staff or chosen departments, enforced on the picker, the URL and the submission API. |
| US-18 Default workflows per report type | Yes | Administration → Workflows. Report Review stays first and Closure Approval last; changes apply to new reports only. |
| US-19 Stages on a report under investigation | Yes | Edit stages on the Workflow tab. Completed and in-progress stages and Closure Approval are locked; dates recalculate; one timeline entry per save. |

US-16 to US-19 are written up in [USER-STORIES-FORMS-AND-WORKFLOWS.md](USER-STORIES-FORMS-AND-WORKFLOWS.md).

## Deliberate decisions worth reviewing

0. **Sign-in is deliberately open** and the ten accounts are published in the README. This is a
   demonstration build with no corporate directory behind it. It is not a security boundary, and
   the permission model it exercises is real — but do not put anything sensitive in it.

1. **Confidential reports drop identity at submission** rather than masking it. Nothing to unmask,
   nothing to leak. Confirm with COFS that no legitimate need to identify a confidential reporter exists.
2. **Gatekeeper closure review is a separate permission** (`closure.gatekeeperReview`) from
   `closure.approve`, which resolves the contradiction between BRD 3.6 and the section 8 matrix.
3. **Attachment limit follows the 25 MB / 10 files figure** from section 6 rather than the 50 MB /
   100 MB figure in the technical notes. One of the two needs to win.
4. **Effectiveness periods follow the section 3.7 list** (includes 1 month and 24 months), not the
   shorter list in SRA field 21.
